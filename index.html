Historical Signals for Identifying Declining Content

Can historical search, traffic, engagement, and content-freshness signals identify content that is currently showing a declining trend—and help prioritize what deserves human review first?

01 · Abstract

Can historical search, traffic, engagement, and content-freshness signals identify content that is currently showing a declining trend? Using 30,000 anonymized content observations, I defined decline from the observed trend_direction label and excluded label-derived fields from the model features. I compared a transparent majority-class baseline with logistic regression under client-grouped validation, using 23,837 training observations and 6,163 test observations with zero client overlap. The grouped test result reached 0.8497 ROC-AUC, 75.61% accuracy, 80.71% precision, 68.69% recall, and 74.22% F1, compared with a 0.50 ROC-AUC majority baseline. The result is best treated as decision-support for prioritizing human content review rather than as a causal or autonomous publishing decision.

02 · Research Question

Can historical search, traffic, engagement, and content-freshness signals identify content that is currently showing a declining trend, and which signals provide the most useful evidence for prioritizing content review?

The goal is prioritization, not automation: identify observations that deserve earlier human attention, then let a reviewer investigate the evidence before acting.

03 · Data

The working dataset contains 30,000 observations and 44 columns. The target was reconstructed as is_declining_label = 1 when trend_direction == "down", otherwise 0. Across the full working dataset, the declining-label rate is 54.21%.

Deliberate exclusions

trend_direction directly defines the target, so it is excluded. trend_pct directly describes the trend used to create the target and is also excluded. Including either would create target leakage rather than independent predictive evidence.

The public-facing report does not expose client names, client-identifying information, private queries, or raw content identifiers. Findings are reported in aggregate.

06 · Interpretation

The strongest practical signals should be understood as evidence for review rather than explanations of why a page declined.

Low search position
Inspect search visibility and content/query alignment.
Recent impression decline
Investigate whether search exposure has weakened.
Recent session decline
Inspect traffic changes and engagement.
Content staleness
Check factual freshness and update need.
No recent impressions
Verify current search exposure before editing.

07 · Limitations & Honest Framing

Important: This analysis does not establish causality and should not be used as an autonomous content-decision engine.

Observational data

The analysis measures associations with an observed label; it does not establish that any feature causes decline.

Recent-performance proximity

Some recent traffic and search variables are close in time to the outcome. Their predictive usefulness may partly reflect information near the labeling window rather than long-horizon forecasting ability.

Client distribution

The grouped test contains seven held-out clients. This is stronger than allowing client overlap, but it remains a limited sample of independent groups.

False negatives

The model missed 986 declining observations in the grouped test set. It should not be treated as a complete monitoring or alerting system.

08 · Ranked Recommendations

Priority 1 — Review prioritization

Route high-confidence model outputs into a human review queue. Require reviewers to inspect the underlying evidence before action.

Priority 2 — Add reason codes

Pair model signals with human-readable evidence such as search-position weakness, impression decline, session decline, or stale content.

Priority 3 — Validate on future client groups

Repeat evaluation on a fresh holdout containing clients and time periods not used during development. Track precision, recall, ROC-AUC, and false-negative rates over time.
