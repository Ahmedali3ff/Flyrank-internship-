01

Research question

Can historical search, traffic, engagement, and content-freshness signals identify content that is currently showing a declining trend — and which signals provide the most useful evidence for prioritizing content review?

02

Problem & decision context

A large content inventory can't be reviewed with equal depth at every moment. The practical goal isn't to automate content decisions, but to surface the observations that deserve earlier human attention. The unit of analysis is a single content/page observation, and the model's output is a binary signal: is the observed trend labeled as declining or not.

03

Data

The working dataset holds 30,000 observations across 44 columns. The target was reconstructed as is_declining_label = 1 when trend_direction == "down", and 0 otherwise. Across the full dataset, the declining-label rate sits at 54.21% — close to balanced.

The model draws on 28 non-label-derived features spanning search demand and competition, content characteristics, historical and recent traffic, engagement, and content freshness.

Deliberate exclusions

trend_direction was dropped because it directly defines the target, and trend_pct was dropped for the same reason — it directly describes the trend the target was built from. Keeping either would create leakage, not independent evidence.

Client and content identifiers are used only for grouping and reproducibility; they're never exposed as public analytical features.

Data safety

The public-facing report exposes no client names, client-identifying information, private queries, or raw content identifiers. Every finding here is reported at an aggregate level from anonymized working data.

04

Methodology

4.1 — Target definition

  • 1 = labeled declining (trend_direction == "down")
  • 0 = otherwise

This is a classification framing, not a claim that the model uncovers some underlying causal state.

4.2 — Leakage checks

Every field derived from the target was explicitly removed. Recent-performance variables stay in the feature set — they're historical signals genuinely available at analysis time — but their closeness to the outcome means their coefficients deserve conservative interpretation.

4.3 — Baseline

The transparent baseline predicts the majority class for every observation in the grouped test set, which holds 3,149 declining and 3,014 non-declining observations — giving a baseline accuracy of 51.09% and a ROC-AUC of 0.50.

4.4 — Model

The primary model is logistic regression with median imputation and feature standardization. It was chosen because its behavior is relatively transparent, and its coefficients give directional evidence about how standardized features relate to the label.

4.5 — Validation design

The final evaluation uses GroupShuffleSplit, grouped by client_id, random seed 42, 20% held out for test.

An earlier random split scored higher, but it let observations from the same client land on both sides of the split. The grouped split is the headline result because it's a more conservative — and more honest — estimate of how the model generalizes to clients it hasn't seen.

5.2 — Random vs. grouped validation

The earlier random split produced 0.9124 ROC-AUC and 83.39% F1. Under client-grouped validation, those figures fell to 0.8497 ROC-AUC and 74.22% F1. The gap suggests random splitting gave an optimistic read on generalization compared with holding entire clients out of the test set.

06

Interpretation

The strongest practical signals are best read as evidence for review, not explanations of why a page declined. The available feature groups cover search visibility, recent traffic, engagement, and freshness.

The action-playbook analysis surfaces useful, human-readable reason codes:

  1. Low search position — inspect search visibility and content/query alignment.
  2. Recent impression decline — investigate whether search exposure has weakened.
  3. Recent session decline — inspect changes in downstream traffic.
  4. Content staleness — check whether the page needs a substantive refresh.
  5. No recent impressions — verify the page still receives meaningful search exposure.

These signals are meant to be read together, not interpreted in isolation.

07

Limitations & honest framing

Observational data

This analysis doesn't establish that any feature causes decline — it measures association with an observed label.

Recent-performance proximity

Some recent traffic and search variables sit close in time to the outcome, so part of their predictive value may reflect proximity to the labeling window rather than genuine long-horizon forecasting.

Client distribution

The grouped test set holds out seven clients. Stronger than allowing overlap, but still a limited sample of independent client groups.

False negatives

The model missed 986 declining observations in the grouped test set — it shouldn't be treated as a complete monitoring or alerting system.

No autonomous action: these results don't justify automatic deletion, publishing, rewriting, or other irreversible content decisions. They support human review prioritization, and nothing more.
08

Ranked recommendations

PRIORITY 1

Use the model as a review-prioritization signal

Route high-confidence outputs into a human review queue. Require the reviewer to inspect the underlying search, traffic, engagement, and freshness evidence before acting.

PRIORITY 2

Combine model evidence with reason codes

Never show a bare score. Pair it with human-readable evidence — search-position weakness, recent impression decline, session decline, or stale content.

PRIORITY 3

Validate on future client groups

Before operational adoption, repeat evaluation on a fresh holdout of clients and time periods not used during development. Track precision, recall, ROC-AUC, and false-negative rate over time.

10

Conclusion

Historical search, traffic, engagement, and freshness signals carry measurable information about the observed declining label. Under the more conservative client-grouped validation, logistic regression reached 0.8497 ROC-AUC and 74.22% F1 — clearly ahead of the majority-class baseline on the same test set. The drop from the random-split numbers is exactly why grouped validation matters when many observations share a client.

The practical value isn't an autonomous content-decision engine. It's a transparent prioritization layer that helps a human decide what to look at first.
